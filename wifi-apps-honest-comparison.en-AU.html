WiFi Aid vs other Wi-Fi tools on iPhone: an honest comparison

When the Wi-Fi drops out again, the question is usually simple: is it the router, the DNS, your internet provider or just one website? There are plenty of iPhone apps that can help, and most are free to download. WiFi Aid is a paid download with no subscription. Here’s how it compares, using only what each app’s Australian App Store page says (checked 27 November 2026, AEDT). We make WiFi Aid, so we’ve kept to what each listing says rather than our own opinion.

Where WiFi Aid is a good fit

  • You want an answer in plain words — “most likely your Wi-Fi / DNS / provider / that website” — with the evidence behind it, rather than raw tools to interpret yourself.
  • Your connection “looks connected but keeps lagging” and you want repeated samples (Deep Check).
  • You have mesh Wi-Fi and want to see which node you’re on and whether you roamed.
  • You’d like to run a check from Apple Watch or glance at a Home Screen widget.
  • You prefer paying once over a monthly plan, and don’t want an account.

Where another app may suit you better (things WiFi Aid doesn’t do)

  • You pay before you can use it. WiFi Aid is a paid download; the other apps above are free to download.
  • No device discovery / “who’s on my Wi-Fi”: WiFiman, Network Analyzer, Fing and NetSpot list this; WiFi Aid doesn’t.
  • No speed test, heatmap survey, port scanner, traceroute or channel suggestions in WiFi Aid’s description — if you need those, the apps above list them.
  • It diagnoses, it doesn’t fix: WiFi Aid tells you the most likely cause; it can’t change router settings or contact your provider for you.
  • Requires iOS 17 or later, and it has no Australian ratings yet, so there are fewer user opinions to go on.
