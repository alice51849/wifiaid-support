How to find WiFi problems on your iPhone: DNS, router and latency checks

The video call freezes, a page keeps loading, and everyone at home blames the router. In reality the fault can sit in your WiFi, your DNS, your broadband provider, or on just one website. Where a home broadband problem can hide

Between your iPhone and a website there are several stages, and a fault at any of them feels like "slow internet". Finding the real cause means ruling the stages out one by one:

CheckWhat it looks atTypical symptom when it fails
DNSWhether a web address resolves to an IP addressWiFi shows connected but pages keep spinning
TCPWhether a connection to the server can be openedSome apps will not connect, or work only now and then
TLSWhether the secure connection completesPages stall halfway or show a secure connection error
Response time (TTFB)How long the server takes to start replyingEvery tap waits a long time before anything happens
Stability (latency variation)Whether latency jumps up and down across repeated samplesLooks connected, feels laggy during calls and games
IPv4 and IPv6Whether both address types connect normallySome sites or services fail far more often than others

Steps to find WiFi problems with WiFi Aid

  1. Run a Check. One tap runs WiFi, DNS and internet checks and shows a result with a diagnosis in seconds.
  2. Read the evidence. DNS, TCP, TLS, TTFB, HTTP, direct IP, IPv4 and IPv6 are listed one by one, so every conclusion has proof behind it.
  3. Use Deep Check for on-and-off trouble. It gathers more evidence and stability samples for problems that come and go.
  4. Check a Website. Type in one address to see whether that single site is failing or the wider internet is.
  5. Compare history. Each result is saved only on your device, so you can compare different rooms and different times of day.

Router, DNS or latency: what to do with the result

WiFi Aid cross-checks the evidence and points to the most likely cause. Use that to decide your next step instead of rebooting at random:

  • Only one website is failing. It is usually that website. Try again later or test on another network.
  • The DNS stage fails. The trouble may be in the DNS setting or the router. Take the result with you when you contact your provider.
  • Latency swings a lot and stability is poor. Run Deep Check in different rooms and at different times to find a pattern in signal or peak hours.
  • Everything fails. The cause may be the router, the modem or the provider's side. The saved history is a useful record to share with support.

WiFi Aid is not a speed test. It reads the evidence it collects and reports the most likely cause; your provider or device maker has the final word on repairs.

Frequently asked questions

WiFi is connected but websites will not open. What is wrong?

Common causes are a DNS lookup failure, a failed secure connection or a problem with the website itself. Run a Check in WiFi Aid and DNS, TCP and TLS are listed one by one, so you can see which stage fails.

How do I check problems that come and go?

Use Deep Check to collect more stability samples and watch the latency variation. Repeat it at different times and compare the saved history.

How do I know if it is my router or just one website?

Use Check a Website with that address. WiFi Aid shows whether one destination is failing or the wider internet is.

Does WiFi Aid send my network data anywhere?

No. There is no account, no ads and no tracking, and check records are stored only on your device.

Can I run a check from my Apple Watch?
